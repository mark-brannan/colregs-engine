# Gemini review of proposal v2 — 2026-09-04

Reviewer: Gemini 3.1 Pro (high) via the agy CLI 1.1.26, headless. Prompt: the v2 reviewer prompt. Verbatim output.

1. **[Serious] §2 Category partition mis-assignments.**
   - **Target:** The row assigning `1(a), 3, 21, 22` to `definition` and `8` to `conduct`.
   - **Finding:** Rule 1(a) is an application clause ("These Rules shall apply..."), not a definition; it belongs in `scope`. Furthermore, you claim `shall-not-impede` is its own modality (Rules 9, 10, 18(d)), yet you grouped all of Rule 8 into `conduct`. Rule 8(f) establishes the `shall-not-impede` obligation and preserves duties once risk of collision exists. The table fails as a sound partition.
   - **Fix:** Move 1(a) to `scope`. Extract 8(f) and assign it to `precedence`.
   - **Source:** Verified from source.

2. **[Blocker] §4 Rule 2 region definition.**
   - **Target:** "A situation s is in the region if, for every own-vessel strategy... there exists an admissible other-vessel behaviour under which a level-1 or level-2 invariant is violated."
   - **Finding:** The definition is malformed. It only proves when compliance is unsafe, not when departure is *necessary* or even helpful. If an adversary is completely unconstrained and aggressively ramming, departure might also fail. To invoke Rule 2(b) as a necessity, there must be a valid escape available outside the rules. The adversary choice is not the biggest open question; your missing existential quantifier is.
   - **Fix:** Conjoin a necessity condition: `AND there exists a non-compliant own-vessel strategy where for all admissible other-vessel behaviours, the invariant is preserved.`
   - **Source:** Expertise (formal verification).

3. **[Serious] §4 Runtime guard rail and abstention.**
   - **Target:** "Silence in the region would be the wrong answer; a labelled abstention is the right one."
   - **Finding:** A decision aid that explicitly abandons the mariner in extremis is a liability. Legally and practically, if your system identifies a Rule 2 region where compliance guarantees collision, merely outputting "rules-insufficient" is dangerous. It leaves the human with zero kinematic support when they need it most.
   - **Fix:** Output the abstention from the *rules*, but supply the safest kinematic escape vector (the winning non-compliant strategy computed by your game solver) as emergency advisory data.
   - **Source:** Expertise (seamanship / safety design).

4. **[Serious] §5 Tractability proxies.**
   - **Target:** "The law already defines the clock; use it rather than inventing thresholds."
   - **Finding:** This is false and uncomputable. Rule 17 defines conditional stages based on spatial geometry ("so close that collision cannot be avoided..."), not a time budget or clock. You cannot read a time budget from Rule 17 without first inventing the very kinematic thresholds you claim to avoid.
   - **Fix:** Acknowledge that the time budget must be computed dynamically from the kinematic time-to-reach the Rule 17(b) spatial threshold.
   - **Source:** Verified from source.

5. **[Serious] §4 Offline computation marked as ink.**
   - **Target:** "Ink: the Rule 2 region is computed offline." (Supported by §5: "relative, 10⁴–10⁶").
   - **Finding:** This lacks evidence to be ink. A relative state space of 10⁶ assumes fixed ship dynamics (acceleration, turn rate). Real vessels have vastly different kinematic capabilities (supertanker vs. speedboat). You cannot precompute a universal Rule 2 region offline that applies to all vessel capability pairs without a combinatorial explosion of the state space.
   - **Fix:** Downgrade to `✎ Pencil` until you either prove offline computation scales across the full parameter space of vessel dynamics, or shift to runtime computation.
   - **Source:** Expertise (data modelling / hybrid systems).
