1. **Blocker — “Every paragraph carries exactly one category.”**  
   **Verified:** Rule 1(b)–(e) are substantive provisions, yet the table omits them while the note says they are “not a category”; Rule 22 is titled and functions as *Visibility of Lights*, not a definition. [USCG consolidated COLREG text](https://navcen.uscg.gov/navigation-rules-amalgamated)

Put 1(b)–(e) and 22 in the table/registry. Reclassify 7(d): it prohibits inference from scanty information, so it is an epistemic conduct constraint, not encounter classification.

2. **Serious — “R1 … ∃ non-compliant own strategy ∀ A: Safe” and “advisories … possibly none.”**  
   **Expertise (formal methods):** Exact R1 entails at least one winning non-compliant strategy; “possibly none” contradicts the status unless it means solver incompleteness. Finite horizon `T` also declares a state safe when collision is merely postponed beyond `T`; a discrete solver may miss controls between cadence points.  
   **Fix:** define R1 only for sound, certified winning policies; otherwise emit `inconclusive-within-model`, not R1/R2. Define terminal controlled-invariant/clearance conditions, continuous-time semantics, strategy observability, and numerical over/under-approximation direction.

3. **Serious — “Which cells humans want to look at is what settles [the adversary].”**  
   **Expertise:** No: `A`, observation model, `T`, separation threshold `d`, action semantics, and formal meaning of “compliant” all determine the set. The proposal supplies no sensitivity evidence for its claim that partial information may move it “more than anything else.”  
   **Fix:** mark that comparative claim pencil; require a parameter-sensitivity matrix, including compliant/arbitrary/mixed adversaries and full/partial observation, before any ink ontology or grid interpretation.

4. **Blocker — `status: rule-2-required`, citing 2(b), plus escape advisories.**  
   **Verified:** Rule 2(b) requires due regard to dangers and special circumstances that *may make departure necessary to avoid immediate danger*; Rule 2(a) preserves the master/crew’s responsibility. [COLREG Rule 2 text](https://navcen.uscg.gov/navigation-rules-amalgamated)  
   **Expertise (seamanship/safety):** A model-derived “required” label dangerously impersonates a legal and navigational conclusion, especially when its full-information, dynamics, and adversary assumptions are false onboard. Input-independence from a human assertion is not a safety guard rail; it can suppress a master’s correction of bad sensing/model classification. Disclaimers do not repair the interface.  
   **Fix:** use `model-detected-rule-conflict` / `no-robust-policy-under-assumptions`; display assumption failures and an explicit abstention for uncertain state, out-of-domain dynamics, or uncertified numerics. Make advisories bounded action envelopes with uncertainty, never imperative manoeuvres.

5. **Serious — “solution measure,” “violation cost,” and “every solution counterintuitive.”**  
   **Expertise:** These are not yet computable, coordinate-independent proxies. Continuous winning-set “size” changes under reparameterization; “cheapest” violations has no defined order or weights; exclusion of two canonical actions does not establish human counterintuitiveness. R2 is not “tier 4 by construction”: it means no robust policy against `A`, not no human recovery.  
   **Fix:** report certified robust-set bounds under a named normalized control metric; replace cost with a Pareto frontier of breached norms; label canonical-action exclusion a proxy, and validate tiers through human-subject/bridge-simulator work.

   **Expertise:** One trace demonstrates neither failure of all compliant own strategies nor R1/R2 membership; TLC is a candidate finder only. Likewise tool names are not evidence that the hybrid, partial-information game is representable or solved soundly.  
   **Fix:** demote tool adequacy to pencil; specify the formal encoding, proof obligations, and a benchmark showing sound region bounds before ink.
